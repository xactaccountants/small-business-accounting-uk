What Are Companies House Filings?

Companies House is the UK register of limited companies. It holds the public record of every company: its directors, its shareholders, its registered office, its share capital and its filings. Every limited company must keep that record up to date, and most changes must be filed within a fixed number of days.

The filings fall into two groups. Annual filings, such as the confirmation statement and the accounts, must be made every year, on time, even if nothing has changed. Event filings, such as appointing a director, issuing shares or changing the company name, must be made when the event happens, usually within 14 days or one month.

Missing a filing has real consequences. The public register shows the filing as overdue, Companies House can fine the company and its directors, and in serious cases it can strike the company off the register. That is why the filings are worth doing on time, every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an accountant for Companies House filings?

No, you can file most Companies House forms yourself online. An accountant is worth using when the filing is complex, when several filings are due at once, or when a mistake would cost more than the help. Restoration, share issues and strike off are the filings where professional help matters most.

What happens if I miss a Companies House deadline?

It depends on the filing. A late confirmation statement leaves the company out of good standing and can lead to a fine or prosecution. Late accounts bring automatic penalties, starting at £150. A late director or share filing is an offence by the company and its officers. File as soon as you can, because the penalty usually grows with how late the filing is.
