Corporate Services > Company Secretary Service

Company secretary services are the professional services that keep a limited company's legal and administrative duties up to date: its statutory registers, its Companies House filings, its board minutes and its confirmation statement. For a small business, that usually means an accountant or a specialist firm takes on the company secretary role, or handles the secretarial work behind the scenes, so the directors can run the business without worrying that a filing has been missed.

What Are Company Secretary Services?

Company secretary services are the ongoing administrative and compliance services that keep a limited company in good standing with Companies House. The person or firm providing them looks after the company's official records and deadlines, files the changes when they happen, and makes sure the public register shows the right information about the company.

The work covers the statutory registers: the register of members, the register of directors, the register of people with significant control and the register of secretaries, where one is kept. It covers filing appointments and resignations of directors, changes of registered office, changes of name and share issues. It covers preparing and filing the annual confirmation statement, and keeping the minutes and resolutions that record the decisions the directors and shareholders have made.

The service is sometimes called corporate secretarial services, and the two phrases mean the same thing.

Does a Small Limited Company Need a Company Secretary in the UK?

A private limited company in the UK does not have to have a company secretary. Since the Companies Act 2006 came into force, private companies have been free to choose whether or not to appoint one, and most small private companies do not have a named secretary at all.

That does not mean the work goes away. The duties that a company secretary used to carry out still have to be done: the filings still have to be made, the registers still have to be kept and the confirmation statement still has to be filed. If there is no secretary, those duties fall on the directors, and the directors are legally responsible for them whether or not they have help.

Public limited companies are different: a public company must have a company secretary, and that secretary must meet qualification requirements set out in the Companies Act.

What Does a Company Secretary Actually Do?

A company secretary is the person responsible for the company's compliance and administration. The role is not about running the business or making commercial decisions. Those are the directors' job. It is about making sure the company's legal paperwork is in order and that Companies House is told about every change.

The secretary keeps the statutory registers up to date. When a director is appointed or resigns, when shares are issued or transferred, when the registered office changes or a person with significant control changes, the registers must be updated and Companies House must be told.

The secretary also prepares the annual confirmation statement, checks that every detail on it is correct as at the confirmation date, and files it within the 14-day deadline. Board meetings and shareholder meetings are part of the role too: the secretary calls the meetings, prepares the agenda, takes the minutes and records the resolutions.

Finally, the secretary is the person who watches the deadlines. Confirmation statements, accounts deadlines, changes to directors and shareholders: the secretary keeps a calendar of them and makes sure nothing slips past.

Who Can Be a Company Secretary?

Almost anyone can be a company secretary of a private limited company, but there are a few rules about who cannot do it. The secretary can be an individual or another company, a corporate secretary. Many small businesses appoint their accountant's firm as company secretary, or appoint a director or a trusted employee to the role. There is no requirement for a private company's secretary to hold a particular qualification.

There are three limits to know. First, the company's auditor cannot be its company secretary. Second, if the company has only one director, that director cannot also be the company secretary. If there are two or more directors, one of them can be the secretary. Third, a person who is disqualified from acting as a director cannot act as a company secretary either.

How Much Does a Company Secretary Service Cost?

The cost of a company secretary service for a small business depends on what is included and how much work the company generates. A basic service, keeping the registers, filing the confirmation statement and watching the deadlines, is usually charged as a fixed annual fee, and for a small company with few changes during the year it is one of the cheaper professional services a business buys.

There is no Companies House fee for filing an appointment or a resignation of a secretary online, so the cost of the service is the professional fee, not a statutory charge. The confirmation statement itself carries its own filing fee, which is payable when the statement is filed. Ask which model your provider uses before you agree, so a busy year does not bring a surprise bill.

The cost should be weighed against the cost of getting it wrong. A confirmation statement filed late leaves the company out of good standing on the public register, and Companies House can fine the company and its directors, and in serious cases can start the process of striking the company off.

What Happens If Your Company Secretary Duties Are Missed?

If the secretarial duties are missed, the consequences fall on the company and its directors, whether or not the company has a named secretary. The most common miss is the confirmation statement. It must be filed within 14 days of the end of the review period, and there is no grace period. Once it is late, the public register shows the statement as overdue, and Companies House can fine the company and its directors. Failing to file is a criminal offence. In the worst case, Companies House can start striking the company off the register.

Missed filings for changes are just as damaging. If a director resigns and the resignation is never filed, the public register still shows that person as a director. If shares are transferred and the register of members is not updated, the company cannot prove who its shareholders are. That causes real problems when the company tries to sell shares, raise money or sell the business.

Out-of-date registers also cause trouble with banks and lenders. A bank opening a business account, or a lender considering a loan, will check the Companies House record and the company's registers. If the two do not agree, the application is delayed while the company sorts its records out.

How Do You Appoint or Change a Company Secretary?

First, the directors agree the appointment, usually by a board resolution recorded in the minutes. The person or firm being appointed must consent to act as secretary, and the company must check that the appointment is allowed: that the appointee is not the company's auditor, and that a sole director is not appointing themselves.

Next, the appointment is filed with Companies House. An individual secretary is appointed on form AP03, and a corporate secretary on form AP04. The filing gives Companies House the secretary's name and address, and for an individual, their date of birth. The company's own register of secretaries is updated at the same time.

To change a secretary, the old appointment is ended and the new one is filed. The outgoing secretary's appointment is terminated on form TM02, and the incoming secretary is appointed on form AP03 or AP04. If a company decides it no longer wants a secretary at all, the appointment is simply terminated on form TM02 and no replacement is appointed. A private company is free to do that, but the secretarial duties still have to be done.

Should You Use an Accountant as Your Company Secretary Service?

For most small businesses, asking an accountant to act as company secretary is the simplest choice. The firm already holds the company's accounts, its tax records and its filing deadlines, so adding the secretarial work means one firm is watching everything. When the accounts are prepared, the firm can check that the registers and the confirmation statement agree with them.

The one thing to check is what the service actually includes. Some accountants include basic secretarial work, the confirmation statement and the registers, in their annual package, and charge extra for one-off filings such as a change of name or a share issue. Others charge the secretarial service separately. Ask for the scope in writing, so the directors know which filings are covered and which ones they still need to arrange themselves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a private limited company have to appoint a company secretary?

No. Since the Companies Act 2006, a private limited company does not have to have a company secretary. The secretarial duties still have to be done, and they fall on the directors if there is no secretary. A public limited company must have one.

Can a sole director also be the company secretary?

No. If the company has only one director, that director cannot also be the company secretary. If there are two or more directors, one of them can be the secretary.

What forms appoint or remove a company secretary?

An individual secretary is appointed on form AP03 and a corporate secretary on form AP04. A secretary's appointment is terminated on form TM02. There is no Companies House fee for filing these appointments or resignations online.

Is a company secretary service worth it for a small business?

Yes, for most small limited companies it is worth it, even though the law does not require a private company to have a secretary. The duties still exist, the directors are still responsible for them, and a missed confirmation statement or an out-of-date register can cost far more than a year's secretarial fee.
