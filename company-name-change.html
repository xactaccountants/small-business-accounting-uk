Corporate Services > Company Name Change

A Companies House change of company name is the formal process a limited company uses to change its registered name with Companies House. You pass a special resolution, file the change with Companies House, pay the filing fee, and wait for Companies House to issue a certificate of incorporation on change of name. Only then is the new name your company's legal name.

What Is a Companies House Change of Company Name?

A Companies House change of company name is the official registration of a new name for a limited company. It changes the company's registered name on the public register, the name shown on Companies House, on your certificate of incorporation, and on the legal documents your company signs.

It is not the same as changing a trading name. A trading name is the name you trade under day to day, and you can use one without changing your registered name. But your registered name is your company's legal name, and only Companies House can change it on the register.

A change of company name does not create a new company. Your company number stays the same, your company keeps its history, its contracts and its tax records, and you do not need to re-register the company. What changes is the name itself, and everything that shows that name needs updating afterwards.

Who Can Change a Limited Company Name in the UK?

For most limited companies, changing the name needs a special resolution passed by the shareholders. A special resolution needs at least 75% of the shareholders who vote to agree to the change.

The directors usually propose the new name and call the meeting or circulate the written resolution, but the shareholders make the decision. If you are the only director and the only shareholder, you still need to record the decision properly. The resolution is the legal record that the change was approved.

There is one exception. If your company's articles of association allow the name to be changed by another method, for example by a decision of the directors alone, you can use that method instead, and you file form NM04 rather than form NM01. Check your articles before you start, because the form you file depends on how the change was approved.

How Do You Check If Your New Company Name Is Available?

Before you pass a resolution or pay a fee, check that your new name is available on the Companies House register. Use the company name availability checker on the Companies House service to search for your proposed name. Companies House will refuse a name that is the "same as" a name already on the register. Even a small difference, such as adding "Limited" or changing punctuation, does not make it a different name in their eyes.

A name can also be refused if it is "too like" an existing name and the other company objects, so do not rely only on the exact-match check. Search for similar names, and think about whether a customer could confuse your company with another business already trading under that name.

Checking first saves you the filing fee and the delay of a refused application. If the name you want is taken, choose another one before you ask your shareholders to vote.

What Are the Rules for Choosing a New Company Name?

A new company name must follow the same rules as a name chosen when a company is formed. Your name must end with "Limited" or "Ltd" if you are a limited company, unless your articles say otherwise. It must not be offensive, and it must not suggest a connection with the government or a public authority unless you have permission.

Some words are sensitive and need approval before Companies House will accept them, for example words such as "Royal", "British", "Bank", "Trust" or "Chartered", and words that suggest the company does regulated work, such as accountancy or financial services, when it does not. If your proposed name includes a sensitive word, you will need to show the approval or permission Companies House asks for.

Your name must also not be misleading. Do not choose a name that suggests your company is larger, older or more established than it is, or that it offers services it does not provide. A clear, honest name is easier to register and easier for customers to trust.

What Are the 6 Things to Check Before You Change Your Company Name?

  1. Is the name available? Search the Companies House register for the exact name and for similar names, and check that no sensitive word in it needs approval you do not have.
  2. Have the shareholders agreed? A special resolution needs at least 75% of shareholders who vote to agree. Record the resolution properly. It is filed with the application.
  3. Do your accounts and records agree? Your name change, your accounts and your tax records should all stay consistent.
  4. Have you checked your trade mark? A name that Companies House accepts can still clash with someone else's registered trade mark. A quick trade mark search before you file can save a dispute later.
  5. What will the rebrand cost? Add up the filing fee, the website and stationery, the signage, and the time to tell customers, suppliers and your bank. The filing fee is the smallest part of the cost.
  6. Is the timing right? Avoid changing your name just before a year end, a tax return or a big contract, when your accounts and filings are already under pressure.

How Do You Change a Company Name Online Step by Step?

Changing a company name online is the fastest route for most small businesses, and it is available when the change is made by special resolution.

First, choose the new name and check it on the Companies House register, as above. Then pass the special resolution, at a general meeting or by written resolution, and keep a copy of it, because you will need to file it with the application.

Next, file the change through the Companies House online service and pay the £20 standard fee, or £85 if you need the same-day service. You will need your company number, your authentication code for the Companies House online service, and the details of the resolution.

Finally, wait for Companies House to process the filing. The change is not complete when you submit the form or when you pay. Your new name takes effect only when Companies House issues the certificate of incorporation on change of name. Keep that certificate. It is the proof that your company's legal name has changed.

If your change is made under your articles rather than by special resolution, you cannot use the online service. File form NM04 by post instead, with the £30 paper fee.

How Long Does a Company Name Change Take?

An online filing is usually processed faster than a paper filing, but there is no fixed time you can rely on. Companies House has warned that filings can take longer than usual when it is experiencing processing delays, so allow extra time and do not promise customers or suppliers a date until the certificate arrives.

A paper application takes longer than an online one, because it has to be posted, opened and entered onto the register. If you need the change quickly, the same-day online service is the faster option, but it still depends on the application being accepted without questions.

Do not start using the new name on invoices, contracts or your website until the certificate has been issued. Until then, your old name is still your company's legal name, and documents signed in the new name before that date can cause confusion about who the contract is with.

What Do You Need to Update After Your Company Name Changes?

Once Companies House issues the certificate, update every place that shows your company's name. Start with HMRC, your bank, and your accountant, so your tax records, your business bank account and your accounts all show the new name. Your company number and your Unique Taxpayer Reference do not change, but the name attached to them does, so tell HMRC and the bank promptly.

Then update your invoices, your website, your email addresses and signatures, your stationery, your signage, and your contracts and terms and conditions. Tell your customers and suppliers in writing, and ask them to update their records so your invoices are paid to the right name.

Also check your next confirmation statement. It must show the company's details as they are on the date you file it, so it should show the new name if the change has taken effect by then. The confirmation statement filing date itself does not change because you changed your name.

Can You Change Your Company Name Back, or Change It Again?

Yes. There is no limit on how many times a limited company can change its name, and you can change it back to your old name if that name is still available. Each change is a fresh process: a new special resolution, a new filing with Companies House, and a new filing fee. You will also need to update your bank, HMRC, your customers and your stationery all over again, so a second change costs as much in time and rebranding as the first.

That is why it is worth choosing carefully the first time. A name that is too narrow can become a problem if the business grows into new services, and a name that follows a short-lived trend can date quickly. If you are unsure, change your trading name first and trade under it for a while. You can change the registered name later, once you are sure the new name will last.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does changing my company name create a new company?

No. Your company number stays the same, your company keeps its history, its contracts and its tax records, and you do not need to re-register the company. Only the name changes.

What is the difference between a registered name and a trading name?

Your registered name is your company's legal name, and only Companies House can change it. A trading name is the name you trade under day to day, and you can use one without changing your registered name.

When does the new name take effect?

The new name takes effect only when Companies House issues the certificate of incorporation on change of name. Do not use the new name on invoices, contracts or your website until the certificate has been issued.

Can I change my company name more than once?

Yes. There is no limit on how many times a limited company can change its name. Each change needs a new special resolution, a new filing, and a new fee.
