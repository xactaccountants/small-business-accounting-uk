How to Restore a Dissolved Company in the UK

Administrative restoration and restoration by court order, explained in plain English.

Corporate Services > Company Restoration

If your company has been struck off and dissolved, it can often be brought back. This process is called restoration of a company. There are two routes: administrative restoration through Companies House, and restoration by court order. The right route depends on who is applying and why the company was dissolved.

What Is Restoration of a Company?

Restoration of a company means bringing a dissolved limited company back onto the Companies House register, so it legally exists again.

When a company is dissolved, its assets usually pass to the Crown as bona vacantia, its bank account is frozen, and it cannot trade, sign contracts, or take legal action. Restoration reverses that, if the application is successful.

What Is Administrative Restoration?

Administrative restoration is the simpler and faster route. It applies when Companies House struck the company off the register, usually because accounts or confirmation statements were not filed.

A former director or member applies, files everything that is missing, and submits Form RT01. If everything is in order, Companies House restores the company without going to court.

This route is often used when a company was dissolved by mistake, or because filings were missed during a busy or difficult period.

Who Can Apply for Administrative Restoration?

You can apply for administrative restoration if you were a director or a member of the company when it was dissolved. The company must also have been trading when it was struck off.

A creditor usually cannot use this route. A creditor who needs the company restored to pursue a claim normally has to apply to the court instead.

What Is Restoration by Court Order?

If administrative restoration is not available, you may need to restore the company through the court. This is known as company restoration by court order.

You will usually need a court order if the company was dissolved voluntarily by its directors, if you are not a former director or member, if more than 6 years have passed since dissolution, or if administrative restoration has been refused.

A court application is more formal, takes longer, often several months, and usually needs legal support. The court will want to see a clear reason why the company should be restored, for example to recover assets, pursue a claim, or correct a mistake.

What Is Form RT01 and When Do You Send It?

Form RT01 is the application form for administrative restoration, sent to Companies House. You send it once the outstanding documents are ready, not before.

Before Companies House will restore the company, all outstanding filings must be prepared: the missing accounts and confirmation statements, and any penalties that are due. If the form is sent with filings still missing, Companies House will not restore the company. File first, then apply, so the application can be dealt with in one pass.

How Much Does Company Restoration Cost?

Restoration by court order carries a court fee of £341 for the Form RT01 application. Administrative restoration has no separate court fee, but you must pay any penalties due and prepare the missing filings, which is where the cost sits.

Getting the route wrong is the most common delay. Many applications are refused because the wrong route was chosen, so confirming eligibility before you file is the step that saves the most time.

How Long Does Company Restoration Take?

Administrative restoration can take several weeks once all documents are filed correctly. The timeline depends on how many years of accounts are missing, and whether the company owes tax or has other outstanding issues with HMRC.

Court restoration usually takes longer, often several months, because it depends on court dates and legal paperwork. Starting early and filing complete, accurate documents is the fastest way to avoid delays.

What Happens to a Company's Assets After Restoration?

When a company is dissolved, its property, money, and other assets normally become bona vacantia, ownerless property that passes to the Crown. Its bank account is frozen.

When the company is restored, those assets usually return to the company. However, this is not automatic in every case, and you may need to take separate steps to recover bank balances, property, or other assets. This is why professional advice matters before you apply, especially if the company owned property, had money in the bank, or was involved in a dispute.

When Should You Get Professional Help With Restoration?

Company restoration is a legal and filing process, not just a form. It is worth getting help if you are unsure which route applies to you, if accounts or confirmation statements are missing for several years, if the company was dissolved more than 6 years ago, if there are assets to recover, or if HMRC or a creditor is involved.

An accountant can prepare the outstanding filings, check the company history with Companies House, and guide you through the right route, so you do not waste time on a refused application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a dissolved company be brought back?

Yes, in many cases it can. Whether through administrative restoration or a court order, a dissolved company can be restored if you follow the correct process and file what is missing.

What is the difference between administrative restoration and a court order?

Administrative restoration is done through Companies House with Form RT01, and it is only available when Companies House struck the company off and the applicant was a director or member. A court order is needed when administrative restoration is not available, for example after a voluntary dissolution or after more than 6 years.

What happens if my restoration application is refused?

Check the reason first. It is often a missing document, an unpaid penalty, or the wrong route was used. You can usually correct the filing and apply again, or consider a court application if administrative restoration is not available. Do not keep resubmitting the same incomplete application. Fix the underlying issue first.

Can a creditor restore a dissolved company?

Yes, in some cases. If a company owed you money when it was dissolved, you may be able to apply to the court to restore it, so you can pursue your claim. Creditors usually need the court route, not administrative restoration.
