Corporate Services > Confirmation Statement

To file your confirmation statement is to tell Companies House, once a year, that the information it holds about your limited company is correct and up to date. You confirm your directors, your shareholders, your registered office, your share capital and the people who control the company, pay the filing fee, and submit the statement within 14 days of the end of your 12-month review period.

What Is a Confirmation Statement?

A confirmation statement, formally known as form CS01, is the annual filing every UK limited company and limited liability partnership must send to Companies House. It replaced the annual return in June 2016: instead of sending a fresh set of details each year, you confirm that the details it already holds are correct, or update them first and then confirm them.

The statement is a snapshot of your company on one date, your confirmation date, also called your statement date. Everything on it must be accurate as at that date: the registered office address, the directors and their details, the company secretary if you have one, the shareholders and how many shares each one holds, the people with significant control, and the statement of capital.

It is not a set of accounts, and it does not report your profit, turnover or tax. It is about who runs and owns the company, where it is based, and how its shares are held.

Who Must File a Confirmation Statement in the UK?

Every limited company registered in the UK must file a confirmation statement at least once every 12 months, including a company that has not traded at all during the year. A dormant company still has directors, shareholders and a registered office, and Companies House still needs those details confirmed.

The duty applies to private and public limited companies and limited liability partnerships, whether anything has changed during the year or nothing has. Sole traders and ordinary partnerships do not file a confirmation statement, because they are not registered at Companies House.

Your accountant can file it for you, but the legal responsibility stays with the directors: if the statement is late or wrong, it is the directors who answer for it.

When Is Your Confirmation Statement Due?

Your confirmation statement is due once every 12 months, and you have 14 days after the end of your review period to file it. Your review period is the 12 months ending on your confirmation date. For your first statement, that date is the anniversary of the date your company was incorporated. After that, it is the anniversary of the date you filed your last confirmation statement.

Check your confirmation date on the Companies House register, put it in your calendar with a reminder a month before, and treat the 14-day window as a hard deadline. The day after it passes, the statement is overdue.

You can also file early, and file more than one statement in a year. Filing early starts a new review period from the date you file, so your next confirmation date moves to the anniversary of that early filing. A second statement in the same 12-month payment period costs nothing extra in Companies House fees.

What Information Does a Confirmation Statement Include?

A confirmation statement covers the company details Companies House holds on the public register, and each part must be correct as at your confirmation date. It includes your registered office address, your directors and whether any director has resigned or been appointed during the year, your shareholders and share capital, and the people with significant control, who own or control more than 25% of the shares or voting rights.

It also includes a statement that your company's intended future activities are lawful. That declaration was added under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, and every confirmation statement now asks you to confirm it.

It does not include your accounts, your turnover, your profit, or your tax figures. If any of the registered details are wrong when you come to file, do not simply confirm them. File the correction first, a change of address, a new director, a transfer of shares, or a change of company name, and then confirm the statement with the corrected details on it.

What Is Identity Verification, and Why Does It Block Your Confirmation Statement Now?

Identity verification is the legal requirement, under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, for the people who run and control UK companies to prove who they are to Companies House. It became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025.

Every director must verify their identity, through GOV.UK One Login or through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider, in most cases your accountant, and gets a Companies House personal code. Existing directors must provide that code with their company's next confirmation statement after 18 November 2025. New directors must verify before they can be appointed at all.

This matters because an unverified director can block the whole filing. Companies House will not accept the statement until every director's personal code has been supplied. The 12-month transition period that started on 18 November 2025 closes on 18 November 2026.

How Do You File a Confirmation Statement Online Step by Step?

Use the Companies House online service, and have your company number and your authentication code ready. First, check each section against your own records. If a section is wrong, stop and file the correction first, then come back to the statement.

Then confirm the lawful activities statement, add the Companies House personal code for each director who needs to provide one, and pay the £50 fee by card. Submit the statement and keep the confirmation email and the filing receipt. Companies House usually accepts an online confirmation statement the same day, and the public register is updated shortly after.

If you do not have your authentication code, request a new one from Companies House well before your deadline. It is sent by post to your registered office, so it takes days to arrive. Do not leave it until the last week.

What Happens If You File Your Confirmation Statement Late?

If you file your confirmation statement late, your company is no longer in good standing, and Companies House can take action against it. The immediate effect is on the public record: the register shows the statement as overdue, and any customer, supplier or lender who looks the company up can see it.

Companies House can also fine the company and its directors, and in serious cases it can prosecute. Failing to file a confirmation statement is a criminal offence. In the worst case, Companies House can start the process of striking the company off the register, which would end the company's existence and freeze its bank account.

There is no grace period after the 14 days. If your statement is already overdue, file it as soon as you can, and sort any unverified director first, because the filing will be blocked until that is done.

When Should You Get Professional Help With a Confirmation Statement?

Most confirmation statements are simple when nothing has changed. Get help if a director has not verified their identity, if shareholders or share capital changed during the year, if the registered office or directors are out of date, or if the statement is already overdue. An accountant can check every section against your records, file the corrections first, and file the statement with the right personal codes, so it is accepted the same day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a confirmation statement the same as annual accounts?

No. A confirmation statement confirms who runs and owns the company and where it is based. Annual accounts report the company's finances. Both are due every year, with separate deadlines, and one does not cover the other.

Does a dormant company need to file a confirmation statement?

Yes. Every limited company must file one at least once every 12 months, including a company that has not traded. A dormant company still has directors, shareholders and a registered office to confirm.

Can I file a confirmation statement early?

Yes. Filing early starts a new review period from the date you file, and a second statement in the same 12-month payment period costs nothing extra in Companies House fees.

What if a director has not verified their identity?

The filing will be blocked. Every director must verify their identity and provide their Companies House personal code with the confirmation statement. Sort the verification first, then file.
