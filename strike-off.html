Corporate Services > Company Strike Off

To dissolve a company in the UK, you normally apply to strike it off the Companies House register. Done correctly, it is straightforward. Done incorrectly, it can leave tax, debts, or filings unresolved. This guide explains when strike off is the right route, how it works, and what to check first.

What Does It Mean to Dissolve a Company?

To dissolve a company means to remove it from the Companies House register, so it no longer legally exists. Once dissolved, the company cannot trade, hold a bank account, or own assets. Any remaining assets usually pass to the Crown as bona vacantia, unless they are dealt with before dissolution.

That is why dissolution is different from simply closing a business down. The company, its directors, and its filings all need to be wound up properly first.

When Should You Strike Off a Company?

Strike off is usually suitable when a company is no longer needed and has no outstanding debts, disputes, or trading activity. Common situations include a company that never traded, a dormant company with no further use, a company formed for a single project that has ended, or a director retiring with no one to take the company over.

If the company has debts it cannot pay, significant assets, or is facing legal action, strike off is usually not the right route. In those cases, a formal insolvency process or professional advice is needed first.

How Do You Strike Off a Company Online?

You can strike off a company online through Companies House, using Form DS01. The basic process is:

  1. Stop trading, and tell HMRC and any other relevant bodies.
  2. Pay any outstanding tax, debts, and filing fees.
  3. Deal with company assets: sell, transfer, or distribute them before applying.
  4. File Form DS01 with Companies House, signed by a majority of directors.
  5. Companies House publishes a notice in The Gazette, and there is a two-month objection period.

If no one objects, and no issues arise, Companies House will strike off the company and publish a final dissolution notice. The company then ceases to exist.

What Is Form DS01, and Who Needs to File It?

Form DS01 is the application to strike a company off the register. It is filed with Companies House, and must be signed by a majority of the company's directors. The form confirms that the company has stopped trading, has no outstanding liabilities that would prevent strike off, and that the directors agree to the application.

You can file DS01 online, or by post. Online filing is usually faster, and Companies House will confirm receipt and publish the Gazette notice to start the objection period.

What Are the 6 Checks Before You Dissolve a Company Online?

  1. Has the company stopped trading? It must not be trading, or planning to trade, when you apply.
  2. Are all tax returns filed? Corporation Tax, VAT, and PAYE must be up to date with HMRC.
  3. Are all debts paid? Creditors can object to strike off if they are owed money.
  4. Have assets been dealt with? Bank balances, property, and equipment should be distributed before dissolution.
  5. Are accounts and confirmation statements filed? Outstanding Companies House filings can delay or block the process.
  6. Have directors and shareholders agreed? A majority of directors must sign Form DS01, and shareholders should be informed.

Missing one of these is the most common reason a strike off is delayed or objected to.

How Long Does a Companies House Strike Off Take?

A Companies House strike off online application usually takes around two to three months in total. That includes the two-month objection period after the first Gazette notice. If an objection is raised, for example by HMRC or a creditor, the process is paused until the issue is resolved.

Starting with clean, complete filings is the fastest way to avoid delays.

What Is the £25,000 Rule When Closing a Company?

The £25,000 rule is about how money taken out of the company before strike off is taxed. Up to £25,000 taken out before strike off can be treated as capital. Over £25,000, the whole amount is taxed as income, not just the extra above the limit.

Three points matter. The £25,000 limit is for all shareholders together, not £25,000 each. The company must actually dissolve within 2 years. And the tax treatment depends on the current tax rates, which should be checked with the tax team before any money is paid out.

Above £25,000, a Members Voluntary Liquidation is usually the better route, because it can treat the distribution as capital. Take advice before paying out near the limit.

What Happens After a Company Is Dissolved?

Once dissolved, the company no longer exists as a legal entity. Its bank account will be frozen and then closed, and any assets left in the company will normally pass to the Crown.

Directors should keep company records for at least six years after dissolution, in case HMRC or another party raises a question. If a mistake is found after dissolution, for example an asset was missed, the company may need to be restored first, through administrative restoration or a court order, before the asset can be recovered.

When Should You Get Professional Help to Dissolve a Company?

Strike off looks simple on paper, but the preparation matters. It is worth getting help if you are unsure whether the company qualifies for strike off, if there are outstanding accounts, tax returns, or Companies House filings, if the company has assets, property, or money in the bank, if a creditor, HMRC, or a former director might object, or if you want the company closed cleanly, with no loose ends.

An accountant can check the company's filing history, settle outstanding tax, and guide you through Form DS01, so the application is not refused or objected to.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between dissolving a company and liquidation?

Dissolving a company through strike off is for a company that is solvent, has no debts it cannot pay, and is simply no longer needed. Liquidation is a formal insolvency process, used when a company cannot pay its debts, or when its affairs need to be wound up by a licensed insolvency practitioner.

Can you dissolve a company that has never traded?

Yes. A company that has never traded is often the simplest one to dissolve, because there are usually no debts, no assets, and no tax complications to deal with. You still need to follow the same process: confirm the company has not traded, make sure no accounts or filings are outstanding, and file Form DS01.

Can a strike off be stopped after you apply?

Yes. If you change your mind, a strike off can be stopped with Form DS02, filed before the company is dissolved. If the company has already been dissolved, it must be restored first.

What happens if someone objects to the strike off?

The process is paused until the issue is resolved. HMRC or a creditor may object if money is owed or filings are outstanding. Deal with the objection, then the strike off can continue.
