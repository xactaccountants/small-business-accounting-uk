How to Appoint a New Company Director in the UK

Appointing a new company director is how a small limited company brings a new person onto its board: a co-founder, a senior employee, an investor, or someone with the skills the business now needs. It is a short process, but it has a strict order: the person must verify their identity first, the appointment must be agreed and recorded properly, and Companies House must be told within 14 days.

What Does Appointing a New Company Director Involve?

Appointing a new director involves three things: a decision inside the company, a filing with Companies House, and an update to the company's own records.

First, the company decides to appoint the person. That decision is usually made by the existing directors, or by the shareholders, depending on what the company's articles of association say. The decision is recorded in a board minute or a shareholders' resolution, and the new director gives their written consent to act.

Second, the appointment is notified to Companies House on form AP01. The form gives Companies House the new director's name, date of birth, nationality, occupation and service address, and it must be filed within 14 days of the date the appointment takes effect.

Third, the company's own statutory registers are updated. The register of directors is updated to show the new director, and the register of people with significant control is checked, because a new director who also holds shares or voting rights may need to be recorded there too.

Who Can Be a Director of a UK Limited Company?

Almost any adult can be a director of a UK private limited company, but there are a few clear rules about who cannot. A director must be at least 16 years old. A company must have at least one director, and at least one director must be a natural person, a real human being, not another company. A company can have corporate directors as well, but it cannot have only corporate directors.

There are people who are not allowed to act as directors. A person who has been disqualified as a director by a court or by the Insolvency Service cannot act as one, and neither can an undischarged bankrupt, unless a court has given permission. Appointing someone who is disqualified is a serious matter: the appointment is not valid, and the person who acts as a director while disqualified can face criminal penalties.

There is no requirement for a private company's director to hold a particular qualification, to live in the UK, or to be a British national. A director can live overseas, and a company can appoint a director who has never run a business before.

Why Would a Small Business Appoint a New Director?

The most common reason is bringing in a co-founder or a key employee. A business that started with one director may need a second person on the board, someone who runs operations, handles sales, or brings technical skills. Making that person a director gives them a formal role in running the company.

Another common reason is investment. An investor who puts money into a company often asks for a seat on the board, so they can see how the business is run and protect their investment. A company may also appoint a director to replace one who has left, or for succession, when a founder plans to step back from the day-to-day running of the business.

Whatever the reason, the same rules apply. The person must be eligible, their identity must be verified, the appointment must be agreed properly, and the AP01 must be filed within 14 days.

What Are the 6 Checks to Make Before You Appoint a Director?

  1. Is the person eligible to be a director? Check that they are at least 16, that they are not disqualified, and that they are not an undischarged bankrupt.
  2. Has the person verified their identity? Since 18 November 2025, a new director must verify their identity with Companies House before they can be appointed. Do this first, not after the board meeting.
  3. Do the articles allow the appointment? Check the articles of association for any limit on the number of directors, or any right for a particular shareholder to appoint a director.
  4. Has the appointment been properly agreed? The board or the shareholders must agree the appointment under the articles, and the decision must be recorded in a minute or a resolution. Get the new director's written consent to act at the same time.
  5. Will the new director be a person with significant control? Work out whether the appointment changes who controls the company, for example if the new director is also being given shares.
  6. Is the 14-day AP01 deadline in the diary? The AP01 must be filed within 14 days of the appointment taking effect. Put the date in the calendar on the day the appointment is agreed.

How Do You Appoint a Director Step by Step?

First, the proposed director verifies their identity. They do this directly with Companies House through GOV.UK One Login, or through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider. Once verified, they receive a personal code, which is given to the company so the appointment can be filed.

Next, the company agrees the appointment. The existing directors pass a board resolution, or the shareholders pass a resolution, depending on what the articles require. The resolution records the new director's name and the date the appointment takes effect. The new director signs a written consent to act.

Then the appointment is recorded. The company's register of directors is updated to show the new director, with their full name, service address, date of birth, nationality and occupation. If the new director is also a person with significant control, the PSC register is updated too.

Finally, form AP01 is filed with Companies House within 14 days of the appointment date, with the new director's personal code. The filing is usually made online, and Companies House usually confirms the appointment within one working day, though this is not guaranteed. The appointment is effective from the date agreed by the company, not from the date the AP01 is filed, but the filing is still a legal duty, and it must be made on time.

What Is Identity Verification, and Why Must It Come First?

Identity verification is the check that a new director really is who they say they are. It was introduced under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, and it became mandatory for new directors on 18 November 2025.

A new director verifies their identity in one of two ways. They can do it themselves, directly with Companies House through GOV.UK One Login, using a passport or other photo ID. Or they can do it through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider, a firm, such as an accountant, that is registered with Companies House to verify identities on behalf of its clients. Either way, the check is a one-off: once a person has verified their identity, they do not need to do it again for a future appointment.

The verification must come before the appointment, not after it. A company cannot lawfully appoint a director who has not verified their identity, and an AP01 filed without the director's personal code will be rejected.

Existing directors are on a different timetable. They have a 12-month transition period, and must verify their identity by the time they file their next confirmation statement. The transition closes on 18 November 2026. A new director has no such grace period: verification comes first, and the appointment follows.

What Is Form AP01, and When Must It Be Filed?

Form AP01 is the form a company files with Companies House to report that it has appointed a new individual director. A corporate director, another company appointed as a director, is reported on form AP02 instead.

The AP01 records the date of the appointment, the new director's full name, date of birth, nationality, occupation and service address, and their personal code from identity verification. It must be filed within 14 days of the date the appointment takes effect, under section 167 of the Companies Act 2006. The same 14-day deadline applies to removing a director, on form TM01, and to changing a director's recorded details, on form CH01.

There is no Companies House fee for filing an AP01 online. It is free, and a paper filing is also free, though much slower.

Getting the AP01 right matters, because the details on it go onto the public register. The new director's name must match their verified identity, and the service address is the address shown publicly. A director who does not want their home address on the public record should use a service address instead. Only the month and year of a director's date of birth appear publicly; the day is not shown.

How Much Does It Cost to Appoint a Director?

The cost of appointing a director is mostly the professional cost of doing it properly, because the filing itself is free. There is no Companies House fee for filing an AP01 online, and identity verification done directly through GOV.UK One Login is also free.

The real costs are the adviser's fee, where one is used, for checking eligibility, preparing the board resolution, updating the registers and filing the AP01, and the fee an Authorised Corporate Service Provider charges for verifying the director's identity, where the company uses one instead of the free GOV.UK route.

A company should also budget for the follow-on work. A new director may need a service agreement, the company's articles may need to be changed if the appointment takes the board above a limit set in them, and a shareholders' agreement may need to be updated if the new director is also becoming a shareholder.

What Happens If You Appoint a Director Incorrectly or File AP01 Late?

If a director is appointed incorrectly, the problem is not only the filing, it is the appointment itself. An appointment made without the authority the company needed, or in breach of the articles, can be challenged, and the director's decisions may be questioned. Appointing a person who is disqualified, or who has not verified their identity, is worse: the appointment is not valid, and a person who acts as a director while disqualified can face criminal penalties.

A late AP01 is a separate offence. The form must be filed within 14 days of the appointment, and failing to file on time is an offence by the company and every officer in default, punishable by a fine. The appointment itself is still valid, but the late filing is visible on the public register.

A wrong AP01 causes a different problem: the public register shows the wrong details for the director. The company's own register of directors will not agree with Companies House, and the error will need to be corrected on form CH01. The original, incorrect filing stays on the register alongside the correction.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old must a director be?

A director must be at least 16 years old. A company must have at least one director, and at least one director must be a natural person.

Can a director live outside the UK?

Yes. There is no requirement for a private company's director to live in the UK or to be a British national.

What is the deadline for filing Form AP01?

Form AP01 must be filed within 14 days of the date the appointment takes effect. The same 14-day deadline applies to removing a director on form TM01.

Does identity verification apply to existing directors too?

Yes, but on a different timetable. Existing directors must verify their identity by the time they file their next confirmation statement, and the 12-month transition period closes on 18 November 2026. New directors must verify before they can be appointed at all.
